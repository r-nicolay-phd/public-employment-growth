Table S23. With a predetermined wage bill shock there is no robust evidence of a delayed output cost
Annual panel 1996–2023, no microstates. Local projections of 100 × log GDP per capita from t − 1 to t + h on a wage bill shock (percentage points of GDP), with two lags of the shock and of growth and country and year fixed effects; h = −1 is zero by construction. Shocks: (a) 100 × (W_{t−1}/P_{t−2} − W_{t−2}/P_{t−2}) / y_{t−2}, dated in t − 1 and deflated by the deflator of t − 2, so it uses no current prices or output; (b) residual of the current shock (100 × (W_t/P_t − W_{t−1}/P_{t−1}) / y_{t−1}) on current growth and two lags, with country-specific slopes and country and year fixed effects; (c) the current shock deflated by the deflator of t − 1. Inference: Driscoll-Kraay standard errors (maximum lag 3); lag-augmented local projections (Montiel Olea and Plagborg-Møller, 2021: one more lag of the shock and of growth, heteroskedasticity-robust errors); half-panel jackknife bias correction (2 × full sample − mean of 1996–2009 and 2010–2023), with the full-sample Driscoll-Kraay standard error. Shock (b) is a generated regressor: its standard errors and 95% CIs come from a country block bootstrap that re-estimates the purging regression in each of 999 replications (percentile CI in the first column; lag-augmented column with robust errors that ignore the generated-regressor step). Panel B: shock (a) interacted with merit above the median in t − 2 (V-Dem), with the total shock as control, so the coefficient is high − low; Romano–Wolf step-down p over h = 0–6, 999 country draws. Stars from the standard errors of each column; 95% CIs in brackets. Exploratory (pre-registered in the plan addendum). * p<0.10, ** p<0.05, *** p<0.01.
