Table S19. Mollick and Cabral's annual coefficient disappears over trend GDP but not with the lagged ratio (approximate replication)
Approximate replication of Mollick and Cabral (2011, Kyklos 64: 122–137), full sample, their column 1. Their 54 industrial and emerging economies (Table 1); dependent variable: log real GDP per capita (WDI, current vintage) on its lag, population growth, log investment/GDP (gross capital formation) and log government size; no time effects, as in the original. Measures: (a) general government final consumption/GDP in the same year; (b) placebo numerator, lagged ratio × (lagged real GDP / current real GDP) × exp(country mean real growth of government consumption), which varies only through the denominator; (c) consumption over Hamilton trend GDP (H = 2, k = 2); (d) the ratio lagged one year; five-year blocks 1986–1990 to 2001–2004 (to 2021–2023 in the extension), log GDP per capita at the end of the block, other variables averaged; (e1)–(e3) the ratio as block mean, first year and last year of the previous block. Fixed effects: country fixed effects, standard errors clustered by country. System GMM: one-step Blundell–Bond, robust standard errors, all regressors endogenous except population growth, instruments lags 2–4 of log GDP per capita and all available lags (2+) of the other endogenous variables, uncollapsed, as in the original (annual, 1986–2004, measure (a): 479 instruments, AR(2) p = 0.593). 95% confidence intervals in brackets; stars from the same standard errors. Panel C: differences between coefficients in a stacked model with country × measure fixed effects, wild cluster bootstrap by country (Webb weights, 9,999 replications), bootstrap 95% CI. Differences from the original: current WDI vintage (constant 2015 USD), no foreign-asset measures, and the published five-year coefficient is not smaller than the annual one in absolute value but loses significance. Results with trade openness (their column 4) in the replication files. Exploratory (pre-registered in the plan addendum). * p<0.10, ** p<0.05, *** p<0.01.
