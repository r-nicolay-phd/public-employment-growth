Table S9. Does meritocratic recruitment moderate the wage bill coefficient? (H3)
Dependent variable: growth of real GDP per capita (%, five-year average). Wage bill and merit (V-Dem v2stcritrecadm; higher values = more merit-based) in t_p − 1; merit is centred at its estimation-sample mean (1.320), so the main effect in column 1 is evaluated at average merit. Predetermined controls of Table 2, country and period fixed effects, standard errors clustered by country. Column 2: binning estimator of Hainmueller, Mummolo and Xu (2019), one model with bin-specific intercepts and slopes and common controls and fixed effects; marginal effects at the median of each tercile; the Wald test compares it with the linear model. Columns 3–7: separate regressions in each tercile or half, each with its own fixed effects (singletons dropped within each subsample), which is why they differ from column 2. Minimum detectable effect: (1.96 + 0.84) × standard error of the interaction. Economically relevant interaction: half of the preferred H1 coefficient (T3) divided by the distance between the 10th and 90th percentiles of merit (−0.101 to 2.349). Threshold models (annual balanced panels, wage bill and merit lagged one year): Seo and Shin (2016), 2015–2019, linearity bootstrap p = 0.912; Hansen (1999), 2000–2019, p = 0.286. In the Hansen panel the linear fixed-effects coefficient of the lagged wage bill is −0.723 without controls and −0.404 with the controls and lagged growth of Table 1, so the large regime coefficients reflect the static specification, not the threshold. * p<0.10, ** p<0.05, *** p<0.01.
