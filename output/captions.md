**Table 2. Public wage bill and growth: baseline estimates**

*Notes:* Dependent variable: growth of real GDP per capita (%, annual average over the period in columns 1, 2 and 4-7; annual in column 3). The wage bill is compensation of employees of the general government (IMF GFS), measured in the first year of each period (columns 1, 2, 4-7) or lagged one year (column 3). Columns 1-2 and 4-5: country and period fixed effects, standard errors clustered by country. Column 3: annual panel with country and year fixed effects, Driscoll-Kraay standard errors; regressors lagged one year. Columns 4-5: government budget constraint, with expenditure shares of goods and services, interest, other expense and either transfers (4) or investment (5), total expenditure and revenue; the fiscal balance is implied. The wage bill coefficient measures a reallocation of one percentage point of spending from the omitted category to the wage bill. Column 6: two-step system GMM (Blundell-Bond) with Windmeijer-corrected standard errors; lagged growth, wage bill, investment and initial income instrumented with lags 2-3, collapsed. Column 7: bias-corrected fixed effects for dynamic panels (Breitung, Kripfganz and Hayakawa, 2022), robust standard errors. Five-year periods 1979-2023, three-year periods 1981-2023, annual 1996-2023; microstates excluded. * p<0.10, ** p<0.05, *** p<0.01.

**Table 3. Employment or pay? Decomposing the public wage bill**

*Notes:* Dependent variable: growth of real GDP per capita (%, five-year average). Public employment is public sector employment as a share of total employment (World Bank WWBI); the relative public wage is the general government wage bill (% of GDP, IMF GFS) divided by the public employment share, i.e. pay per public employee relative to GDP per worker; the public wage premium is the WWBI conditional public-private wage gap with respect to all private employees (log points). WWBI variables are survey-based: the first observation available within each period is used. Country and period fixed effects; standard errors clustered by country. Turning point = -b1/(2 b2), with a 95% confidence interval from the delta method. Five-year periods, 1999-2023 (WWBI starts in 2000); microstates excluded. * p<0.10, ** p<0.05, *** p<0.01.

**Table 4. Does bureaucratic recruitment moderate the growth cost of the wage bill?**

*Notes:* Dependent variable: growth of real GDP per capita (%, five-year average). Wage bill: general government compensation of employees (% of GDP) in the first year of the period. Meritocratic recruitment: V-Dem v2stcritrecadm (criteria for appointment decisions in the state administration; higher values = more merit-based), measured in the first year of the period. WGI: government effectiveness (higher values = more effective), first year of the period (from 1996). A positive interaction means that the growth cost of the wage bill is smaller where the moderator is higher. Columns 3-7 split the sample at the median and terciles of merit in the estimation sample of column 1; the equality test comes from a pooled model with all coefficients and the period effects interacted with the group. Controls as in Table 2. Country and period fixed effects; standard errors clustered by country. The threshold estimates (Seo and Shin, 2016) are in Table A6. * p<0.10, ** p<0.05, *** p<0.01.

**Table A6. Threshold models with meritocratic recruitment as the regime variable**

*Notes:* Dependent variable: annual growth of real GDP per capita (%). Strongly balanced annual panels (required by both estimators), microstates excluded. Columns 1-2: dynamic panel threshold model of Seo and Shin (2016), first-difference GMM (xthenreg), asymptotic standard errors; the lower-regime slope and the change in the slope above the threshold are shown.
